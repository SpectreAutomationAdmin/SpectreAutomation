Executive Operations Centre — three concepts for the flagship

Three high-fidelity 1440×900 static mockups exploring different compositions for the Admin Dashboard as the club's executive operations centre. All three share the same underlying design system — warm-charcoal shell, Source Serif 4 display type, gold-hairline detailing, per-club accent — and diverge on hierarchy, information architecture, and signature composition.

Not production. No React, no migration, no design-system propagation. These are static HTML mockups intended to reach the creative bar before any code is written. All Silver Springs figures are exemplar — a real build would source from ClubProfile + the live projections. Once you select or combine concepts, only then does implementation begin.

The Council Table
Five columns · Roman numerals · italic conditions · one focus per domain

Metaphor. The mahogany conference table at a Board meeting — a formal placement of each domain side by side, presented for review. No hierarchy imposed; equal ceremony given to each pillar.

Signature elements. Roman numerals (I–V) in gold-700; italic serif "conditions" as one-liners under each domain header ("Attentive — two applications await…"); trailing-decimal treatment on currency ($5,902 large + .50 muted); a single per-column "Focus" affordance that names one action; gold hairlines top + bottom + between columns; no cards, no icons, no colored badges.

Intended emotional response. Ceremony. Boardroom formality. Confidence expressed as calm restraint.

Why it is not a conventional SaaS dashboard. No card containers, no icon column, no coloured status pills. The layout reads as printed stationery, not software chrome. Roman numerals are unheard of on a dashboard.

  • Feels like Spectre?YesThe typographic five-column arrangement + italic conditions read as proprietary from first glance.
  • Recognisable without logo?YesThe Roman numerals + gold hairlines + italic serif conditions are unmistakable.
  • Signature momentThe italic serif condition under each column header — "Attentive — two applications await your decision by Friday" — no other dashboard writes like this.
  • Premium enough for executive users?YesReads as club stationery on club letterhead.
  • What still feels generic?The value stack in each column is still label→value→label→value. The "Focus" affordance is clever but risks repetition across five columns.
  • Compromises madeDensity is deliberately low — each column shows 3 numerics. Some information density from the pre-migration dashboard was traded for calm.
  • Would a customer remember it after one viewing?Yes"The club dashboard that reads like a Board-meeting agenda in serif type."
Concept B Open full 1440 ↗
The Situation Wall
Lead domain featured · secondary bays with top-lip stripes · queue ribbon

Metaphor. A private-club situation room. One domain takes the wall today — the one that most needs attention. Others compress into secondary bays. A ribbon of "today's queue" chips runs beneath.

Signature elements. The "LEAD DOMAIN · MEMBERSHIP" tag paired with a large editorial state statement ("Two applications require decisions before Friday."); a single primary CTA that names the specific action ("Review the two applications →"); top-lip accent stripes (3px) in domain-specific colours (green / gold / slate / burgundy / terracotta); a bottom queue-chip ribbon with domain-colour dots.

Intended emotional response. Alert, attentive, in-charge. Duty officer at the wall.

Why it is not a conventional SaaS dashboard. Asymmetric hierarchy — one domain dominates. The state statement is one editorial sentence, not a KPI. The queue chips read as intent, not filters.

  • Feels like Spectre?YesThe lead-domain paradigm + editorial state statement are distinct.
  • Recognisable without logo?PartialThe featured bay + state statement is unusual, but the four secondary card bays read close to conventional dashboard grids.
  • Signature moment"Two applications require decisions before Friday." — a serif editorial sentence in place of a headline number.
  • Premium enough for executive users?YesFeatured bay feels editorial and specific.
  • What still feels generic?Four secondary bays as a 2×2 card grid is closer to convention. Top-lip stripes are refined but similar patterns exist elsewhere (Linear, Notion).
  • Compromises made"Lead domain" only works when there IS one. Quiet mornings need a fallback lead so the featured bay doesn't feel manufactured.
  • Would a customer remember it after one viewing?Partial"The dashboard with a big featured story and small tiles" — memorable, but the pattern exists elsewhere.
Concept C Open full 1440 ↗
The Club Statement
Document paradigm · masthead + editorial opening · roman-numeral ribbons

Metaphor. A private-banking client's monthly portfolio statement — but delivered fresh each morning. The dashboard is a document. Volume XVIII, Reading No. 195. An editorial opening paragraph. Horizontal ribbons per domain. Very subtle trend indicators.

Signature elements. The masthead ("Statement of the Club · Volume XVIII · Reading No. 195") — no other SaaS product identifies itself as an issue. The editorial opening paragraph with gold-highlighted key phrases ("two open applications", "two board decisions"). Horizontal domain ribbons with Roman numerals as the anchor. Extremely subtle trend indicators (▲ green up, ● neutral, ▲ gold attention).

Intended emotional response. Private-banking gravitas. Stewardship. Confident restraint.

Why it is not a conventional SaaS dashboard. Reads as a document, not a UI. No CTAs. No cards. No coloured badges. The "action" is in the prose — "two open applications that require your decision before Friday" — the reader knows what to do because they read.

  • Feels like Spectre?Yes — stronglyThe document paradigm is the most differentiated of the three.
  • Recognisable without logo?Yes"Statement of the Club · Volume XVIII" is unmistakable. No other SaaS opens like this.
  • Signature momentThe masthead paired with the editorial opening paragraph — the whole workspace is a signature moment. Second: the gold-highlighted keyphrases inside prose.
  • Premium enough for executive users?YesPrivate-wealth-statement gravitas.
  • What still feels generic?The horizontal ribbons risk reading as table rows once you focus on them. The Roman-numeral column is distinctive but might feel decorative on a repeat visit.
  • Compromises madeZero direct action affordances. The intent is that prose tells the executive what to do — but a busy admin may want a click-through, not a paragraph.
  • Would a customer remember it after one viewing?Yes — strongly"The dashboard that reads like a private wealth statement."

My candid take

Concept C is the most creatively ambitious and the most memorable — it feels genuinely proprietary in a way A doesn't quite reach and B doesn't attempt. But it carries a real UX risk: no action affordances. Concept A is the safest bet — proprietary and operational, but slightly closer to a "beautifully typeset dashboard" than a paradigm shift. Concept B is the weakest of the three creatively; the secondary bays pull it back toward convention.

  • If forced to pick one: Concept C — but only if we introduce a single, restrained action affordance inside the prose paragraph (e.g. the highlighted key phrases become actual links, and a small "Today's actions" ribbon appears above the footer). That preserves the document paradigm while restoring operability.
  • If hybridising: C's masthead + editorial opening + Roman numerals, layered over A's five-column council table (not C's ribbons) for the operational body, with B's specific-action CTA ("Review the two applications →") appearing once, at the bottom of the workspace, tied to the most urgent open item.
  • What I would not recommend: shipping B as the flagship. It's the closest to what I built the first time — an incremental improvement on a card grid, not a paradigm shift.
